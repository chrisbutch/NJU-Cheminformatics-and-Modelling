### **How often is the class offered?**
The class is offered every **Fall** semester.

---

### **What is this course actually about?**
It is a crash course in physics-based computational chemistry for drug design - docking, molecular dynamics, free energy methods, and a little electronic structure theory. It is **not** a machine learning course. The premise is that you need methods whose assumptions you understand in order to check the ones you don't, before committing real experimental resources.

If you are looking for machine learning applied to drug discovery, that is the **AI for Medicine** course.

---

### **Do I need to have taken AI for Medicine first?**
No. The two courses are independent and there is very little overlap after Class 1, which covers the representation and file-format basics this course needs.

---

### **What background do I need?**
A working knowledge of **Python** and undergraduate physical chemistry will make the course much easier. You should be comfortable reading code and modifying it, though you will not be asked to write software from scratch. No prior experience with docking or simulation is assumed.

---

### **What software will we use, and do I need to install anything?**
The class is designed to use free cloud resources. Software will be installed on remote servers, though students may use their own hardware if they choose. 

---

### **What if my computer doesn't have a GPU?**
Coursework is provided on **Kaggle** which provides every user with  30 hours of free GPU time per week, covering the molecular dynamics exercises. Docking and tight-binding calculations run acceptably on CPU. You will not be asked to run a simulation that requires hardware you do not have access to.

---

### **What is the expected workload?**
Plan on **1–2 hours outside of class per week** for assignments, with more in the weeks leading up to the project presentation.

---

### **How is the course graded?**
- **Exam**: In class, worth 40% of the final grade.
- **Final Project**: Worth 40% of the final grade, presented in the final session.
- **Homework and Class Participation**: Worth 20% of the final grade.

---

### **What is the final project?**
You will take a set of compounds against a target, rank them with a cheap method, then escalate the top of that ranking through docking, molecular dynamics, and free energy estimation. The deliverable is an account of **where the ranking changed and why** — a well-diagnosed disagreement between two methods is worth more than three methods agreeing.

Full guidelines are posted separately. You will have a supervised work session before presentations.

---

### **How much help is available outside of class?**
A class **WeChat group** is used for announcements and questions to the instructor or TA. Office hours are available by request.

---

### **Is this useful for a career in China?**
Yes. Computational chemistry groups exist in essentially every pharmaceutical company operating in China, and the domestic industry is shifting from generics toward novel targets — which is exactly where structure-based methods earn their keep. The skills also transfer to materials science, catalysis, and any field where you need to reason about molecular interactions.
